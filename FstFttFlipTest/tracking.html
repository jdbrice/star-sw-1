10. FTT hits on the track: the planar measurement runs for the first time (2026-10-01)

Every campaign up to here ran fttNoAdd=1: the tracker searched for sTGC hits and filled the diagnostics, but never put one on a track. So the planar FTT measurement had never executed in the Kalman fit. This run turns that on. It differs from the iteration-2 campaign in one field (DIAG "2,1,0"→"2,0,0"), same 90 files and same 13000 events each, which makes iteration 2 an exact control arm. 90/90 jobs clean.

Correction: most of that deficit is the design acceptance gap (2026-10-01)

The quadrant table above uses all tracks as the denominator, which bakes in the sTGC design acceptance gap below the beampipe. FST covers nearly 2π while the four sTGC pentagons cluster above and to the sides of the beampipe, so FST-seeded tracks projecting into the gap can never pick up a hit and the raw number is diluted by geometry working as designed.

The existing histograms could not separate the two, because h2FttProjXY is filled only inside the loop over FTT points — i.e. only for tracks that already matched. Adding the missing denominator (h2FttProjAllXY, the projection of every track to each plane) and restricting to cells where the detector demonstrably is:

  • The factor 2.3 raw deficit becomes ~1.2 (bot/top ~0.81) once the design gap is removed, and pickup inside the active area is ~0.21–0.34 rather than ~0.20.
  • No evidence of a geometry or mapping fault in quadrants B and C. The hit-side map (StFttDb, {0,3,2,1}) and the projection-side map (FwdGeomUtils::fttChamberIndex, (4-q)%4) are identical, raw FttRawHit counts are comparable in all four quadrants, and the bottom quadrants match at 66–97% of the top rate wherever they are instrumented. A mis-mapping would give near-zero matching there, not that.
  • The residual ~20% top/bottom difference is small enough to be edge effects, the leftover misalignment, or statistics (one file, 4000 events) and is not pursued here.
  • Caveat on the method: "active" cells are those where matching already happens, so a region where a quadrant never matched would be excluded rather than flagged. The raw-occupancy and index-mapping checks above are what cover that case.

χ2 triples, and that is the real result

  • Primary χ2 mean 4.33 → 16.74 (+286%), rms 10.2 → 35.1. Not fit failure — the FTT hits disagree with the FST+vertex fit by far more than their stated precision claims.
  • The FTT measurement sigma is the cluster sigma alone (StFttClusterPointMaker.cxx), with no alignment term. FST in the same codebase does the opposite: σtotal = sqrt((pitch/√12)² + δalign²) with δalign,r = 1.5 cm.
  • With the true track-to-hit scatter at 3.7–4.8 mm, each FTT hit contributes ~(δ/σ)² ≈ 7.5 to χ2, matching the observed +12.4 over 1.65 hits. The 47% purity contributes as well, since a wrong hit is far from the track.
  • Inflating the FTT sigma in quadrature would make χ2 look healthy, but it would hide the misalignment it absorbs. The leftover per-quadrant offsets still reach 0.46 cm and the ~1% radial scale is ~0.6 cm at r = 60 cm, both comparable to the scatter — so the χ2 is arguably reporting the geometry, not the covariance.

Why these residuals must not feed the next alignment iteration

With hits on the track the Primary blind residual is self-biased. The Global refit puts FTT hits on the global seed, and the primary fit reuses that seed, so the Primary projection has already been fit to hits from the same disks. At identical entry counts the blind dy peak width collapses:

  control   0.373  0.400  0.440  0.477 cm
  hits-on   0.232  0.208  0.207  0.236 cm

That is self-fitting, not better alignment. Alignment therefore stays on fttNoAdd=1, which is what iteration 2 used. An unbiased residual with hits on the track is still available from fttDiagType=0 (Global), whose seed is FST-only until its own refit.

12. The FCS residual, with and without aligned FTT hits (2026-10-03)

Two Run 24 zero-field campaigns that differ in one thing: whether the aligned sTGC hits are put on the track. Same iteration-1 geometry (fGeom.y2024.iter1.sdt20240622.root), same 90 input files, 647 117 events in each. Primary tracks, projected to the ECAL and matched to FCS clusters.

  • arm A — DIAG 2,1,0, NOADD=1: FTT hits searched but never added to the track.
  • arm B — DIAG 2,0,0, NOADD=0: aligned FTT hits on the track.

Mixed-event is normalised to same-event over |d| = 80–200 cm and subtracted, so what is plotted is the correlated part only.

The residual is a core on a wide tail, not a Gaussian

A single Gaussian does not describe it: the fitted σ follows whatever window it is given — 5.8 cm at ±30 cm, 3.5 cm at ±10, 1.9 cm at ±3 for the same histogram. Fitting a narrow and a wide Gaussian together over one wide window gives a number that does not depend on where we cut, and that is what is quoted below. Only ~38% of the correlated area is in the core; the rest is a ~10 cm tail that survives mixed-event subtraction, so it is real same-event structure — wrong-cluster pairing and overlapping showers — not combinatorics.

What this means for using FCS as an anchor

  • A core of 2.4–3.2 cm is workable. MC predicts √(1.2² + 1.5²) ≈ 1.9 cm from the cluster position resolution and the track projection, so the data sit 1.3–1.7× above expectation — close enough that FCS can serve as the downstream anchor point.
  • The 62% tail has to be handled explicitly, by the core+tail fit or a tighter matching cut. A plain mean or RMS of the matched sample is dominated by it.
  • Primary is 1.4–1.5× better than BLCVtx on the same events. Where a TPC vertex exists, use Primary; the forward stream, which mostly has none, pays that factor.
  • The sTGC is not the lever here. Finishing the sTGC alignment buys ~11% on the core, not a step change — the FCS cluster position and the projection over the long lever arm dominate.

13. Reconciling with the 2026-07-06 result (2026-10-04)

Many things changed at once between the 2026-07-06 trackmatch page and section 12: run year, beam conditions, track type, the magnetic field, the geometry switches, the tracking code, and the binning. This section separates them, one variable at a time, with nothing un-fixed — the old histogram files were kept, and they carry the same names and the same six track types as today, so the old result is re-measured rather than re-run.

stepwhat changeshow
1track type, BLCVtx → Primary both types live in every file, old and new
2run year, Run 22 ZF → Run 24 ZF two existing campaigns, same code
3geometry switches, GEOFIX 1,1,1 → 1,0,0 two arms on the same fwd_stream data, same code
4tracking code, and the magnetic field a third arm at B=0, to split code from field

Track type is the largest single effect

Core σ of the FCS residual, BLCVtx → Primary, measured inside one file so nothing else moves:

  • Run 22 ZF: 7.40→4.04, 7.05→4.19, 4.15→3.39, 3.61→3.27 cm
  • Run 24 ZF: 7.18→4.03, 7.30→4.50, 3.57→2.83, 4.22→3.21 cm

Primary is 38–45% better than BLCVtx in dx and 9–24% in dy, in both run years. This is why the forward stream, where most events have no TPC vertex and only BLC/BLCVtx are available, is not directly comparable to a zero-field result that uses Primary.

Run 22 and Run 24 agree

Same code, same track type, same FTT setting: 4.04/4.03, 4.19/4.50, 3.39/2.83, 3.27/3.21 cm. Scattered within the method spread, with no trend. The raw distributions agree to under 1%.

Two things to know before reading any of these numbers

  • Mixed-event subtraction is not always valid. It is only a background model where same/mixed is flat. It is flat for all three fwd_stream datasets (1.15–1.16) and for Run 22 ZF (1.22), but for Run 24 ZF it slopes from 1.18 to 1.08, so one scale factor over-subtracts near the peak. Check the flatness before subtracting, and compare raw distributions when it fails.
  • Binning is not neutral. Fitting the same data at 4 cm instead of 0.5 cm inflates the fitted core by a consistent ~7%, so anything compared against the old files is first put on the old 4 cm axis.

The rest: the magnetic field, and the code

RMS in cm. Each column changes one thing and the three factors multiply to the net, so the old and current results are now fully accounted for:

  • GEOFIX 1,0,0 gains 15–20%, the largest and cleanest of the three.
  • The real magnetic field costs 7–16%. That is the price of being right, not a regression: the narrower B=0 number came from extrapolating every track as a straight line, which carries no momentum-dependent projection error because it ignores the momentum altogether.
  • The code change costs 6–37%, at matched field and geometry switches. dx is 6% and ECAL Top dy is 37%. This one is not understood and is the open item from this reconciliation; the dy panels carry nearly all of it. FTT time calibration has been excluded as the cause: per-run anchors for 10 of the 11 runs, re-run end to end, move every panel by 2% or less.

Net, the current chain is 4–7% better than 2026-07-06 in dx and 3–34% worse in dy, and the dy loss is the code term above rather than anything to do with the run year, the beam conditions or the track type.
