SUPERSEDED — the contaminated FST/FTT flip measurement (2026-09-21)

The first section of this page is wrong and is kept only for the record (the second, "Corrected measurement", is the sound but small preliminary that replaced it). addFttHits() APPENDS the sTGC hits it finds to the track's seed, and refitTrack() runs once per track type in sequence, so only the first (Global) refit is genuinely FST-blind — every later type was "matched" against sTGC hits already in its own fit. On top of that the diagnostic had no track-type filter, so one physical track entered up to five times with different constraints. The excess and purity numbers below are dominated by that circularity, and the FST conclusion drawn from them — that the wedge mirror barely matters — is an artefact of it.
→ Go to the current page (blind campaign, 2026-09-22).
→ The same contamination affects the July study, real_data_excess_check.html.

Three geometry corrections, tested one at a time on zero-field data (run 23063028): gap = applyFstGapFix, FSTm = applyFstMirror, FTTm = applyFttXYMirror. Configurations are named g<gap><FSTm><FTTm>.

What is measured

Everything comes from the FST-blind diagnostic: the track is fitted without sTGC hits, projected to each sTGC plane, and the sTGC hits near that projection are histogrammed before any hit is attached to the track.

Which tracks? The diagnostic is filled inside addFttHits(), which runs from refitTrack() — and that is called once per TRACK TYPE. So excess and purity mix Global, BLC, Primary, FwdVtx and BLCVtx: the same physical track enters up to five times, fitted with different constraints. Only the usage fraction below is a single type (Global). This matters: for one track measured here, the five types projected to sTGC radii from 31 to 49 cm, because the FST-only fit is free to choose the polar angle while the vertex-constrained fits are not.

  • excess — height of the peak at the projection point over the local sideband background (peak |d|<0.6 cm, sideband 0.6-3 cm). 1.0 means no correlation at all. V and H are the two strip orientations: V supplies the precise x, H the precise y. Both are given; they are independent measurements of the same thing.
  • purity — background-subtracted peak entries divided by all matched entries: of the hits the tracker would attach, the fraction that are the genuinely correct one.
  • FTT used — fraction of Global tracks (this one IS type-selected) with a hit used on an FTT plane ("hit pickup probability"), top and bottom quadrants separately.
  • FST residual — the FST's own r·dφ residual, split at r=16.5 cm into the inner sensor (which the gap fix does not touch) and the two outer sensors (where the gap term lives), plus the residual mean folded into one 30° wedge ("wedge modulation"): a wrong wedge orientation should show a 30°-periodic pattern.

What it says

  • The FTT X/Y mirror is the dominant effect, and it is harmful. Turning it off takes purity from 23% to 33%, and the excess from 1.84/1.51 to 2.28/2.05. With it ON, the two FST switches make no difference at all (g111 = g101 = g011 to within 0.2%): it swamps everything.
  • All three off is better still, 33.1% → 37.9%. So the FST gap fix and wedge mirror together cost about 5 points of purity. Which of the two is responsible is what g100 and g010 are for.
  • Hit pickup probability barely moves (17% top, ~5% bottom everywhere). The tracker attaches about the same number of sTGC hits in every configuration — what changes is how often the attached hit is the right one. Pickup probability alone would have told us nothing; purity is the discriminator.
  • This agrees with the July measurement (real_data_excess_check.html), which also favoured the non-flipped FTT, and it contradicts the mirror-histogram reading made on 2026-09-16. The direct A/B is the one to trust: actually running with the mirror off exercises clustering, time calibration and point building, while the diagnostic mirror only re-maps a coordinate afterwards.

Caveats

  • The matching metric involves both detectors, so a wrong FST transform could in principle be compensating a wrong FTT one. The FST-internal residuals point the same way, which makes that less likely, but they are not fully independent either: FTT hits also enter the Global fit, which is why g110 and g111 differ slightly in FST RMS.
  • The gap fix was originally justified on field-on data reconstructed with the old B=0 field bug (χ²/ndf 3.2% better, 8.5% more hits surviving). That configuration is now known to have been broken, so the original evidence needs redoing in this setup.
  • The FST residuals come from tracks fitted to those same FST hits (3 hits, straight line at zero field), so they are correlated, not unbiased residuals.
  • Zero field only. Nothing here has been checked with the magnet on.

Consequence

Every production campaign of 2026-09-15/16 ran with fttmirror=1 — the setting this test says is wrong. They are still valid for the time-window study (all four campaigns there share the same flags), but the absolute matching numbers on the timecut page would be better with the mirror off.

Is the FST mirror actually doing anything? Yes — and that is the puzzle

g110 (mirror ON) and g000 (mirror OFF) give nearly the same match quality, which is hard to believe if every FST hit really moves by up to 15° inside its wedge. Checked directly, by printing each hit as read from the MuDst and as used by the tracker (diagnostic build, same file, same events):

 mirror=0 gap=1 disk0 wedge 0 sens1  r 20.81  phi_in  87.273  phi_out  88.273  dphi   1.000 deg
 mirror=1 gap=1 disk0 wedge 0 sens1  r 20.81  phi_in  87.273  phi_out  63.727  dphi -23.547 deg
 mirror=1 gap=1 disk0 wedge 2 sens1  r 17.94  phi_in  27.273  phi_out   3.727  dphi -23.547 deg
 mirror=1 gap=1 disk0 wedge10 sens1  r 26.56  phi_in 137.898  phi_out 133.102  dphi  -4.797 deg

So the mirror is applied and it is large: hits move by up to 23.5°, about 8 cm at r=20 cm. FST hits are uniform in wedge phase (measured: 8-10% per 2.5° bin across the full ±15°), and only 4.9% of hits move by less than 1.3°, so this is not a case of most hits sitting near the wedge centre where the mirror does nothing.

Yet:

  • track yield barely changes: 18.20 → 17.54 tracks/event;
  • FST residuals barely change (RMS 0.069 → 0.079 cm on disk0 inner);
  • the sTGC match peak stays narrow and centred at zero, and purity falls only 37.9% → 33.1%.

The first two are expected: a reflection about the wedge bisector is an isometry, so a real track maps to a perfectly good straight-line track, and the FST fit cannot tell the difference. The third is not. A mirrored track points 10-25° away in azimuth at the sTGC, so its own hit should be nowhere near the projection, and the peak should collapse to the accidental level. It does not. Either the diagnostic is not measuring what we think for these tracks, or something downstream of the FST partly undoes the reflection. This is unresolved and it is the reason not to act on the FST part of this test yet. The FTT X/Y conclusion does not depend on it: the FTT mirror was OFF in both g000 and g110.

Corrected measurement: genuinely blind tracks (2026-09-21, later)

This section is the first, one-file (3000 event) blind measurement. Its method is right and its conclusions were confirmed, but the numbers are superseded by the 80-job campaign on the current page.

Two problems were found with the measurement above, both in the diagnostic rather than in the geometry switches:

  1. Circular matching. addFttHits() adds the sTGC hits it finds to the track seed, and refitTrack() is called once per track type in sequence. The Global refit is blind; BLC, Primary, FwdVtx and BLCVtx all inherit sTGC hits from it and are then "matched" against hits already in their own fit. That is why mirroring every FST hit by 10-25° appeared not to matter: the projection was anchored by sTGC hits, not by the FST.
  2. All types mixed. The diagnostic has no track-type filter, so the same physical track enters up to five times with different constraints (measured: sTGC projection radii from 31 to 49 cm for one track).

Both were fixed with diagnostic switches: fill for ONE track type, and never add sTGC hits to any track (search and histogram only). The natural choice is Primary: FST hits plus the TPC primary vertex, which is an independent constraint — it comes from neither detector under test. What the corrected numbers say

  • The peak is real when it is there. Event mixing — the same tracks searched against the PREVIOUS event's sTGC hits — gives 0.3-0.4% purity and excess consistent with 1.0. So a surviving peak is genuine per-particle correspondence, not detector acceptance.
  • The FST mirror does hurt, as the geometry always said it must: 10.2% → 3.5%. Mirroring moves hits by up to 23.5° (~8 cm at r=20), so a real correspondence cannot survive it. The earlier "mirror barely matters" result was entirely the circularity.
  • All switches off remains the best configuration, now for a defensible reason.
  • The absolute correspondence is weak: about 10% even at best, on tracks constrained by the TPC vertex. The 23-38% purities quoted earlier in this page, and in the July study, are inflated by circular matching and should not be used.

Health warning on everything measured with this diagnostic

The same circularity applies to the FTT time-window study on the timecut page. The comparison there is between window settings at fixed track treatment, so the RELATIVE conclusion (a tight window is better) is not overturned, but the absolute purity numbers are inflated for the same reason. The FTT X/Y mirror conclusion also survives in the corrected data (g111 is the worst configuration in the Primary-blind row), though with much smaller absolute numbers.

  • Make the blind behaviour a real option rather than a diagnostic env switch, so the tracking code can be validated against it.
  • g100 / g010 (gap fix alone, mirror alone) are still needed, now under the blind treatment.
