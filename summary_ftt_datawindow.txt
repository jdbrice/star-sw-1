FTT data windows -- where this stands, and what is still ours to do
===================================================================
(working notes, not the package README)

The table
---------
Calibrations_ftt.fttDataWindowsB. Today it holds two legacy unstamped rows at
2021-10-01 00:00:00 and 00:00:01, written Nov 2025: uniform [-65,100], anchor 0.
INSERT is granted, so nothing is blocked on the DB team -- but Dmitry warned of
a system + perl upgrade that can break master->slave sync, so hold the writes
until that settles.

Writes go to robinson.star.bnl.gov:3306; reads come from
star-db01.sdcc.bnl.gov:3316 (user loader).

Entries go at RUN START MINUS 10 SECONDS, one second apart, 11 slots per run
(start-10 .. start). Every event of a run is at or after its start, so an entry
in that window serves the whole run, while an entry AFTER the start leaves the
run's first seconds on the previous run's entry. Ten seconds is safe: over 2149
runs (2022, days 060-099) the smallest gap between one run ending and the next
starting was 26 s. fttDataWindow_db.C's "update" option takes the next slot and
refuses once they are used, pointing at the DB team for a deactivation.

min/max is production's time cut
--------------------------------
StFttClusterMaker runs with mTimeCutMode = 0 (kTimeCutModeDB), so whatever is in
min/max IS the cut applied in BFC. The afterburner is the exception: it sets
mode 2 with its own fttTimeCutLow=-40, fttTimeCutHigh=100, so afterburner and
BFC disagree today (-40 vs -65). That should be brought into line, probably by
having the afterburner default to the DB window. Still open.

Moving the table from [-65,100] to one bunch crossing takes signal-kept from
1.00 to about 0.83 while making it roughly 8x cleaner. That is a physics choice
to make deliberately, not a side effect of calibrating the anchors.

The window is now measured, and it only opens
---------------------------------------------
fttDataWindow.C fits the in-time crossing per VMM against a same-phase pileup
background (t + k*4.263, k = +-1..+-6) and grows the window outward from the
peak until it holds 95% of that crossing. The result is then widened, never
tightened, to contain [-1,3]. Rationale: per-VMM, per-run, a few hundred events;
too wide costs a little pileup, too narrow throws away real hits in production,
silently.

Measured, 200 events each (stored window; the fit alone is in QA cols fitLo/fitHi):

  23081049  2022 field on, st_fwd       356 [-1,3]   2 [-2,3]   1 [-3,3]  1 [-5,3]
  24192016  2023 AuAu                   346 [-1,3]   8 [-2,3]   2 wider low
  24192017  2023 AuAu                   347 [-1,3]   7 [-2,3]   3 [-3,3]

AuAu works: 384k raw hits in 200 events of 24192017, 357 of 384 VMMs calibrated,
converged. It is not FTT-poor data -- 59% of its events simply have no FTT
points at all (sTGC not read out), and the other 41% carry a median of 852.

Regenerating data/
------------------
The 56 committed files carry the old uniform [-65,100]. To remake them:

    condor_submit condor/submit_fttcal_regen_window.txt    # from OUTSIDE singularity
    script/mergeFttCalQA.sh                                 # then check every stop reason

All 56 runs are queued, on the same data they were calibrated from: the 13
zero-field runs keep the exact MuDst named in their header, and the 43 field-on
2022 runs read 4 st_fwd MuDsts each.

**Do not use get_file_list.pl to find the Run 22 fwd-stream MuDsts.** The file
catalog knows only the P24ia st_fwd files, which are HPSS-only, and its P23if
st_physics entries are stale -- of 160 it lists on NFS for 23081049, exactly one
is still on disk. The data is in the forward DEV reconstruction, which the
catalog does not index at all:

    /star/data2[0-3]/reco/production_pp500_2022/ReversedFullField/DEV_fwd_test2/...
    /star/data23/reco/production_pp500_2022/ReversedFullField/DEV_fwd_test2_freeze/...

17621 st_fwd MuDsts over these 43 runs, 18 to 588 per run, every run covered.
List that area from disk.

anyway, with this macro.

One copy of the macros
----------------------
fttDataWindow.C and fttDataWindow_db.C live in
StRoot/StFttDbMaker/macro/FttDataWindows. script/ and ftt_sim_hit_maker/timecut/
hold symlinks to them, so the web page's download link and the condor jobs all
pick up the same file. runfttcal copies through the symlink, and now also takes
a <run>.list input (it used to take the run number from an st_*.MuDst.root name
only).


VMM 54, and what it exposes (2026-10-16)
----------------------------------------
akio asked why VMM 54 of run 23081049 was fitted [-5,0]. It is not a shifted
peak and not a wrong anchor. It has NO TIME STRUCTURE AT ALL.

Counts per tick around the FOB anchor, 200 events:

  vmm 52   51  6  7 35 60 11 10 102 241  80 37 20 60 26 19  1 38
  vmm 53   57 18 14 50 55 16 10 147 305  85 23 37 92 45 10 10 67
  vmm 54   33 26 20 38 30 27 23  27  60  60 45 37 44 47 47 39 38
  vmm 55   48 11  3 24 58 18  7  70 207  64 19 14 50 28  7 11 25

Its three FOB mates have a sharp peak (241/305/207 over a 10-60 background)
and the neighbouring crossings visible at +-4. VMM 54 is flat, 23 to 60 across
the whole range, on comparable statistics (6996 hits against 6903/9022/5704).
Its fitted window has S/B 0.68 -- it admits more background than signal --
against 3.21 for VMM 53.

Consequences:

  - Its anchor is meaningless rather than wrong. The stored 1075 differs from
    its FOB's 1070 because findAnchor's 4-bin rebin had to choose between two
    groups holding 174 and 173 counts. The raw argmax is 1070, same as the FOB.
  - The existing QA cannot catch this. A VMM is accepted on its in-window
    FRACTION, and with a 166-tick window [-65,100] a flat distribution scores
    the same as a peaked one: VMM 54's 0.589 sits right on the run median
    0.571, so it passes with status 0.
  - Anchors are constant per FOB, and 344 of the 360 calibrated VMMs do have
    exactly their FOB median. The 16 that do not are anchor-finding errors:
    offsets +1 (x8), +2, +3, +5 (vmm 54), +6, +9, +12, -1, -3. fobTol = 20
    ticks was fine for a 166-tick window and is far too loose for a 5-tick one.

TO DECIDE (not changed yet, both affect what production gets):
  - reject a VMM whose fitted window has S/B below some value, give it anchor
    -1 and the wide window, and add a status code for it;
  - snap an accepted anchor to its FOB median, or at least tighten fobTol from
    20 ticks to 1-2 now that the window is 5 ticks wide.

Open and NOT working: script/checkFttVmmChannels.C was written to ask whether
VMM 54 is a few noisy channels or the whole chip. It runs but reads only a
handful of events -- the per-channel statistics are far too thin to conclude,
and its anchors disagree with the per-VMM ones. Needs debugging and many more
events before it says anything.
