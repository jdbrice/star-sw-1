FTT data windows -- where this stands, and what is still ours to do
===================================================================
(working notes, not the package README)

The table
---------
Calibrations_ftt.fttDataWindowsB. Today it holds two legacy unstamped rows at
2021-10-01 00:00:00 and 00:00:01, written Nov 2025: uniform [-65,100], anchor 0.
INSERT is granted, so nothing is blocked on the DB team -- but Dmitry warned of
a system + perl upgrade that can break master->slave sync, so hold the writes
until that settles.

Writes go to robinson.star.bnl.gov:3306; reads come from
star-db01.sdcc.bnl.gov:3316 (user loader).

Entries go at RUN START MINUS 10 SECONDS, one second apart, 11 slots per run
(start-10 .. start). Every event of a run is at or after its start, so an entry
in that window serves the whole run, while an entry AFTER the start leaves the
run's first seconds on the previous run's entry. Ten seconds is safe: over 2149
runs (2022, days 060-099) the smallest gap between one run ending and the next
starting was 26 s. fttDataWindow_db.C's "update" option takes the next slot and
refuses once they are used, pointing at the DB team for a deactivation.

min/max is production's time cut
--------------------------------
StFttClusterMaker runs with mTimeCutMode = 0 (kTimeCutModeDB), so whatever is in
min/max IS the cut applied in BFC. The afterburner is the exception: it sets
mode 2 with its own fttTimeCutLow=-40, fttTimeCutHigh=100, so afterburner and
BFC disagree today (-40 vs -65). That should be brought into line, probably by
having the afterburner default to the DB window. Still open.

Moving the table from [-65,100] to one bunch crossing takes signal-kept from
1.00 to about 0.83 while making it roughly 8x cleaner. That is a physics choice
to make deliberately, not a side effect of calibrating the anchors.

The window is now measured, and it only opens
---------------------------------------------
fttDataWindow.C fits the in-time crossing per VMM against a same-phase pileup
background (t + k*4.263, k = +-1..+-6) and grows the window outward from the
peak until it holds 95% of that crossing. The result is then widened, never
tightened, to contain [-1,3]. Rationale: per-VMM, per-run, a few hundred events;
too wide costs a little pileup, too narrow throws away real hits in production,
silently.

Measured, 200 events each (stored window; the fit alone is in QA cols fitLo/fitHi):

  23081049  2022 field on, st_fwd       356 [-1,3]   2 [-2,3]   1 [-3,3]  1 [-5,3]
  24192016  2023 AuAu                   346 [-1,3]   8 [-2,3]   2 wider low
  24192017  2023 AuAu                   347 [-1,3]   7 [-2,3]   3 [-3,3]

AuAu works: 384k raw hits in 200 events of 24192017, 357 of 384 VMMs calibrated,
converged. It is not FTT-poor data -- 59% of its events simply have no FTT
points at all (sTGC not read out), and the other 41% carry a median of 852.

Regenerating data/
------------------
The 56 committed files carry the old uniform [-65,100]. To remake them:

    condor_submit condor/submit_fttcal_regen_window.txt    # from OUTSIDE singularity
    script/mergeFttCalQA.sh                                 # then check every stop reason

All 56 runs are queued, on the same data they were calibrated from: the 13
zero-field runs keep the exact MuDst named in their header, and the 43 field-on
2022 runs read 4 st_fwd MuDsts each.

**Do not use get_file_list.pl to find the Run 22 fwd-stream MuDsts.** The file
catalog knows only the P24ia st_fwd files, which are HPSS-only, and its P23if
st_physics entries are stale -- of 160 it lists on NFS for 23081049, exactly one
is still on disk. The data is in the forward DEV reconstruction, which the
catalog does not index at all:

    /star/data2[0-3]/reco/production_pp500_2022/ReversedFullField/DEV_fwd_test2/...
    /star/data23/reco/production_pp500_2022/ReversedFullField/DEV_fwd_test2_freeze/...

List that area from disk.

One copy of the macros
----------------------
fttDataWindow.C and fttDataWindow_db.C live in
StRoot/StFttDbMaker/macro/FttDataWindows. script/ and ftt_sim_hit_maker/timecut/
hold symlinks to them, so the web page's download link and the condor jobs all
pick up the same file. runfttcal copies through the symlink, and now also takes
a <run>.list input (it used to take the run number from an st_*.MuDst.root name
only).


VMM 54: a real but weak peak, lost by the anchor finder (2026-10-16)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
akio asked why VMM 54 of run 23081049 was fitted [-5,0], and read the jump from
~25 to 60 as possibly real. It IS real. Counts per tick in its FOB's frame
against a local level of 25 (median of the 70 ticks outside |t|<=5):

   t      -4   -3   -2   -1   +0   +1   +2   +3
   counts 30   27   23   27   60   60   45   37
   sigma 1.0  0.4 -0.4  0.4  7.0  7.0  4.0  2.4

7.0 sigma at t=0 and again at t=+1, 9.9 sigma for the two together. Not a
fluctuation -- just weak: contrast 2.4 against 14 for its FOB mate VMM 53. And
akio's "1 tick late compared to the others" is right: its mates peak sharply at
t=0 with a -1 shoulder, while 54 is flat across 0 and +1.

WHAT WENT WRONG WAS THE ANCHOR, not the window fit. findAnchor used Rebin(4),
i.e. windows aligned to multiples of 4, and VMM 54's peak straddles a group
boundary: dbcid 1070,1071 are the last two bins of their group, so it summed
170 while two neighbouring groups holding only the broad tail summed 173 and
171. The anchor came out 1075, five ticks late, and the fit then chased the
real peak to [-5,0]. The raw argmax was 1070 all along, the same as its three
FOB mates.

Fixed by dropping the grouping altogether: findAnchor is now the plain argmax
over single dbcid values. That is also what the CONSUMER does --
HitCalibHelper::add() keeps a running argmax, "the most populated dbcid" -- so
the macro now really has the definition it always claimed.

Grouping was meant to help when statistics are thin. Measured, it does not.
Counting the VMMs whose anchor disagrees with the median of their FOB mates,
plain argmax vs a sliding 4-tick sum:

  23081049  pp field on, 4063 hits/VMM    17 / 360  vs  17 / 360
  24192017  AuAu,        ~1000 hits/VMM   29 / 354  vs  29 / 354
  25174011  zero field,  7206 hits/VMM    88 / 344  vs  88 / 344
  25174011  zero field,   522 hits/VMM    92 / 330  vs  93 / 330

Identical everywhere but the thinnest case, where the plain argmax is slightly
better. The one VMM the two versions disagree on, 130 of run 23081049, is on a
board whose other three VMMs are all rejected on 234-378 hits; the plain argmax
rejects it too, which is the right call.

Run 23081049 then stores [-1,3] for VMM 54, exactly as akio asked, and for
every calibrated VMM in the run: 360 of 360, no outliers left at all (was
356 [-1,3] + 2 + 1 + 1). AuAu 24192017: 355 [-1,3], 2 [-2,3].

RESOLVED, THE OTHER WAY. I had suggested snapping an off-FOB anchor to its FOB
median and tightening fobTol from 20 ticks. Both would be WRONG. Checked every
one of the 15-17 VMMs whose anchor differs from its FOB median on run 23081049:
each has its own sharp, well-populated peak at the shifted position.

  FOB  9   36,39 peak at t=0 (165,181)     37 at +2 (282)   38 at +3 (289)
  FOB 43   174,175 peak at t=0 (141,169)   173 at +1 (322)  172 at +5/+6 (105,185)
  FOB 94   377,378,379 at t=0              376 at +9 (116)
  FOB 15   60,62,63 at t=0                 61 at +1 (426 against 110 at 0)

Even the off-by-one cases are decisive peaks, not a coin flip between two bins.
And per channel (fttDataWindow.C's vmmQA argument) the offsets are WHOLE-CHIP:
VMM 372 is 12 ticks late in all 9 of its live channels, 376 nine ticks in 7 of
10, 61 one tick in 52 of 56. Not noisy channels, not a search failure -- a
per-VMM hardware timing offset.

Snapping would keep only 80% of what is in the window today over those VMMs,
and 22% for VMM 172, 34% for 376, 36% for 374, 41% for 372.

So fobTol = 20 is right as a sanity check: it exists to catch an anchor wrong by
hundreds of ticks, not to enforce agreement. A tighter one would reject good
calibrations. Worth noting too that "the FOB median" is ill-defined when two of
four differ -- on FOB 9 it follows the shifted VMMs 37,38 and makes 36,39, which
sit exactly on the board anchor, look wrong.

The one thing worth doing is recording the offset as QA, so a VMM 12 ticks
(300 ns) off its board mates is visible to whoever cares about the hardware.

ALSO STILL OPEN. The acceptance test cannot reject a VMM with no real peak: a
VMM is accepted on its in-window FRACTION, and with a 166-tick window a flat
distribution scores like a peaked one. VMM 54 is not that case -- its peak is
real -- but the gap stands, and the fitted window's S/B (0.68 for VMM 54 at the
wrong anchor, 3.21 for VMM 53) is the natural discriminator now that we measure
the crossing.

The per-channel question is now answered inside the package rather than by a
separate script: fttDataWindow.C takes a vmmQA list of up to 8 VMMs whose dbcid
it also keeps per channel, and fttDataWindowPlot.C draws it. The earlier
standalone script/checkFttVmmChannels.C is deleted -- it duplicated the MuDst
reading and never worked.
