sTGC survey: what STAR_LARGE_DETECTOR_IN_IP_REPORT.pdf can and cannot give us
2026-10-01   source: ~/www/fcs/survey/STAR_LARGE_DETECTOR_IN_IP_REPORT.pdf (4 pages)

The PDF has no extractable text (32 chars via ghostscript txtwrite); it was read by
rendering to PNG at 150 dpi and reading the tables off the images. Numbers below are
transcribed by eye -- re-check any single value before it goes into a DB table.

STRUCTURE
  Page n = RIB n = sTGC station n. Each page carries
    - a Point Group  (3MM_1..20, RIBn_1..10)  x,y,z in mm
    - eight plane fits: quadrants A,B,C,D, each FRONT and BACK
      (page 3 calls them "G10 FACE/BACK PLANE 3x", page 4 "4x FRONT/BACK PLN")
  Plane form is A*x + B*y + C*z + D = 0 with B ~ +-1, so the plane sits at y = -D/B.
  The survey's Y is the beam axis; the detector sits at |y| ~ 3100-3650 mm.

WHAT IS DIRECTLY USABLE

1. z per station per quadrant -- YES, this is the headline.
   Chamber thickness comes out 35.2-38.9 mm everywhere, which is what validates the
   plane interpretation. The BACK plane is the one that matches our geometry:

     station   survey BACK mean   our AGML z    diff
       1          3122.20 mm      3123.42 mm   -1.22 mm
       2          3300.57         3299.53      +1.04
       3          3476.47         3476.37      +0.10
       4          3654.17         3654.22      -0.05

   Sub-mm to 1.2 mm on all four. Per-quadrant z, referenced to quadrant A (mm):

     station      A        B        C        D
       1        0.00    -1.71    -0.89    -3.29
       2        0.00    -3.37    -5.47    -4.77
       3        0.00    -2.49    -3.88    -4.29
       4        0.00    -0.19    -1.96    -0.92

   This is exactly the quantity we carry as t2 in pentOnStation.

2. Chamber tilt per station per quadrant -- YES, and we currently ignore it.
   Normal (A,B,C) gives tilt_x = A/B, tilt_z = C/B. Largest total tilt 8.21 mrad
   (0.47 deg, station 2 quad A), i.e. a sagitta of ~4.9 mm across a 60 cm half-
   chamber. Every Survey_st row we write today has an identity rotation matrix, so
   this information is unused. For a track at eta~3 (tan ~0.1 to the beam) a 4.9 mm
   z error maps to ~0.5 mm transverse -- small, but not negligible against the
   2-5 mm residuals we are chasing.

WHAT IS *NOT* IN THIS DOCUMENT

3. x,y per quadrant -- NO. A plane fit carries orientation and one offset along its
   normal; it has no in-plane position. Nothing in these eight fits per page can say
   where a chamber sits transversely.
   The point groups do not rescue this: 3MM_* and RIBn_* are targets on the support
   RIB (the octagonal frame in the drawings), one set per station, not per quadrant.
   They could give a station-level transverse position, but only against nominal
   design positions for each target, which this document does not contain.
   To get x,y per quadrant we need either the drawing with nominal fiducial
   positions, or a survey of features on the chambers themselves.

COMPARISON WITH OUR FITTED t2 (pentOnStation.20211112.000003)
   Both referenced to quadrant A, mm, (ours - survey):
     station 1:  B -0.06   C +0.64   D +0.85
     station 2:  B +4.78   C +3.17   D +2.55
     station 3:  B +1.55   C +0.97   D +2.36
     station 4:  B +0.06   C +0.85   D +1.25
   rms 1.35 mm, max 4.78 mm, mean +1.58 mm.
   Stations 1 and 4 agree to ~1 mm; stations 2 and 3 drift.

   CAUTION, this is not an independent check. Our t2 was built from StFttDb's
   hardcoded per-quadrant z, which itself came from a survey (#707 used quadrant A's
   survey value). So a disagreement is more likely a transcription or convention
   problem inside StFttDb than new physics.

   Quadrant labelling is NOT the problem: testing all 24 permutations of the survey
   letters against our t2, the identity mapping ranks 2nd of 24 and the best
   (BACD) is only marginally better (rms 1.25 vs 1.35). No permutation stands out,
   so survey A,B,C,D matches our A,B,C,D. The data does not discriminate strongly,
   so do not read the ranking as proof either.

OPEN QUESTION WORTH ASKING THE sTGC PEOPLE
   Our z matches the BACK plane, not the midplane. The chamber is ~36 mm thick, so
   if the active gas plane is actually the midplane we are off by ~18 mm -- far
   larger than everything above. Worth confirming which surface "the chamber z"
   is meant to be before any of this is used.

DATE OF THIS SURVEY, AND WHAT TO ASK FOR FOR RUN 24   (added 2026-10-01)

The footer "SA 2022.1.0308.2 ( x64 )" is the SpatialAnalyzer software build (release
2022.1, build 0308), NOT the measurement date. The document's own metadata settles it:

    /CreationDate (D:20220513105538-04'00')   ->  2022-05-13 10:55:38 EDT

So this is a Run-22 era survey. Note it post-dates the Run 22 ZF data we compare it
with (2022-03-04) by about two months; assumed immaterial, not established.

A Run 24 survey is worth requesting: our Run 24 data says the sTGC sits ~1 cm from
where Run 22 left it (see pentOnStation.20231210.000001), and a survey is the only
independent check on that.

BUT A REPEAT OF THIS DOCUMENT WOULD NOT TEST IT. The per-quadrant plane fits give the
position along the chamber normal (z) and the two tilts, and nothing else: a plane
carries no in-plane position. Our ~1 cm is in x and y. The 3MM_*/RIB*_* point groups
do not rescue it either -- they are targets on the support rib, one set per station,
with no nominal design positions in the document.

So ask for:
  1. the same per-quadrant FRONT/BACK plane fits -- directly comparable to 2022, and
     they test the z and the tilts; plus
  2. something that pins the chambers TRANSVERSELY: fiducial/target coordinates
     together with their NOMINAL DESIGN positions, or measurements of features on the
     chambers themselves rather than on the rib.
Without (2) a Run 24 survey confirms z and tilt and leaves the x/y claim untested.
