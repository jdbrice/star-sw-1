StFstDb and StFttDb class functions

Functions added for the forward upgrade, on top of what is in main. Ranges below are inclusive. All of these return a status (bool false, or -1) rather than a silently wrong value when an index is out of range or the geometry is not loaded.

Watch the units. Strip geometry and the quadrant offsets are in mm; everything that goes through TGeo -- quadToGlobal, localToGlobal, globalToLocal and the FST sensor-local pair -- is in cm. agmlQuadLocalToDbLocal is a pure rotation and does not rescale, so it is in whatever the caller used.

Where the silicon actually sits in z   cm   a wedge is mounted FRONT or BACK, alternating, and the parity is inverted on disk 2
diskNominalZ (static) disk(1-3)double [cm]; 0 if out of range 151.750 / 165.248 / 178.781, the zdisk values from FstmGeoSM.xml. This is the front/back MEAN, so no sensor is actually at it.
wedgeOrientation (static) disk(1-3), wedge(1-36)+1 front (+z) / -1 back; 0 if out of range Which face of the disk the wedge is mounted on, as ±kFstzFilp[disk-1]*kFstzDirct[iwedgem] — the same two StFstConsts arrays the phi logic uses, so z and phi cannot drift apart. The leading sign comes from fstmConfig below. Back wedges also carry a 180 deg rotation about x in the XML.
setFstmConfig (static) config: 2 = as installed (default), 1 = pre-mirrorvoid Which way round the wedges are mounted, same meaning and same two values as FSTG_fstmconfig in FstmGeoM/SM.xml. 2 is the real detector (AGML wholewedge 2.0, modulus2 {1,0,1,0,...}), built by y2022c / ft2022c; 1 is the parity of every geometry before 2026-10-25 (wholewedge 1.0, {0,1,0,1,...}), needed to reconstruct samples made on y2022-y2025, dev2021, dev2022, dev2022m, dev2022sm or dev2026. The two differ by 2 x 1.7419 cm (inner) and 2 x 0.3469 cm (outer) at all 36 wedges, and by 0.0000 cm in x and y. Any other value is refused with a warning and the setting is left unchanged.
fstmConfig (static) noneInt_t: 1 or 2 The value in force; 2 (as installed) unless setFstmConfig has been called. Verify a sample with script/checkSensorZvsGeom.C(cache, config), which agrees 108/108 on the matching geometry and reports 108 "wrong face" with a worst |diff| of 3.4838 cm on the other.
sensorZOffset (static) disk(1-3), wedge(1-36), sensor(0-2)double [cm], signed Offset of the silicon from the disk nominal: -+1.7419 for the inner sensor (0) and -+0.3469 for the two outer halves (1, 2), sign from wedgeOrientation. Both are XML expressions, confirmed by measurement to 0.3 mm.
sensorZ (static) disk(1-3), wedge(1-36), sensor(0-2)double [cm] diskNominalZ + sensorZOffset. This is what a hit's z should be; StFstHitMaker now uses it instead of the bare disk nominal.
Strip -> position, the one implementation   moved verbatim out of StFstHitMaker so the producer, the slow simulation and the MuDst rebuild cannot drift apart
stripR (static) rStrip(0-7)double [cm]; 0 if out of range kFstrStart[rStrip] + 0.5*kFstStripPitchR, i.e. the middle of the R strip. 5.000 to 22.100 cm in steps of 2.875 cm (the R pitch).
stripPhi (static) disk(1-3), wedge(1-36), sensor(0-2), rStrip(0-7), phiStrip(0-127, double) double [rad]; 0 if out of range The wedge edge from kFstphiStart/kFstphiStop (start and stop swap on disk 2), stepped by phiStrip in the direction kFstzFilp[disk-1]*kFstzDirct[module-1], with ±0.5*kFstStripGapPhi (1 deg total) for the two outer halves. Inner vs outer is decided by rStrip, not by sensor — kept exactly as StFstHitMaker always did it; the two disagree for a few hits per thousand. phiStrip is double because clustering makes it a charge-weighted mean.
stripPosition (static) disk(1-3), wedge(1-36), sensor(0-2), rStrip(0-7), phiStrip(double), xyz[3] out bool; xyz[3] [cm] cartesian, nominal frame (stripR*cos(stripPhi), stripR*sin(stripPhi), sensorZ), so the z follows fstmConfig. False if any index is out of range. An outer rStrip carried on sensor id 0 has no phi in this convention and is placed on the beam line (0, 0, sensorZ) with a warning, which is what StFstHitMaker has always done.
Which survey row applies to a wedge   Geometry/fst/hssOnFst has one row per HALF and Geometry/fst/fstWedgeOnHss one row per WEDGE — both used to be read from row 0 and applied to all 36 wedges
halfId (static) wedge(1-36)half(0-1), else -1 The row of hssOnFst for that wedge. 0 is the x>0 half (modules 1-6, centre azimuth +75 to -75 deg), 1 is x<0. Taken from the wedge azimuth, cos(centre) > 0, not from a wedge-number cut: AGML's own cut is iwedgem.ge.6 (0-based) but the pre-generated geometry the release ships still has .ge.5 and splits the disk 5/7 instead of 6/6. The two tables differ by 2 x (0.0775, -0.016) cm, so a wrong half is 1.55 mm in x.
getGeoHMatrixHssOnFst half(0-1), from halfIdconst TGeoHMatrix* [cm] Half-of-support-structure on FST. Run 22 translations are (-0.0775, +0.016, 0) and (+0.0775, -0.016, 0) cm, rotations identity.
getGeoHMatrixWedgeOnHss wedge(1-36)const TGeoHMatrix* [cm] Wedge on half. Identity in every lane we carry; this is the table a wedge-level alignment would be written to.
Sensor local <-> global   cm
sensorLocalToGlobal (static) wedge(1-36), sensor(0-2), sensor-local xyz[3][cm]bool; global xyz[3][cm] Composes the loaded survey matrices, fstOnTpc * hssOnFst[half] * fstWedgeOnHss[wedge] * fstSensorOnWedge, all four in cm. tpcOnGlobal is NOT in the product: AGML misaligns FSTM in CAVE with Geometry/fst/* only, so including it moved the hit 2.4/1.7/2.0 mm (plus 0.8 mm from the TPC's -0.48 mrad over the 165 cm z lever arm) out of the frame of the GenFit plane. Run 22 totals are (+0.0713, -0.3010, 0) cm for half 0 and (+0.2263, -0.3330, 0) cm for half 1. False if the index is out of range (wedge 1-36, sensor 0-2) or the geometry has not been loaded.
globalToSensorLocal (static) wedge(1-36), sensor(0-2), global xyz[3][cm]bool; sensor-local xyz[3][cm] The inverse of the above.

Also private: moduleFromSector(sector(0-11)) -> module(1-12), built from kFstphiStart/kFstphiStop.

GEANT g2t volume_id -> StFttDb numbering   volume_id = 100*station(0-3) + 10*pentagon(0-3) + layer(1-2). What g2t calls "plane" is the PENTAGON; StFttDb's "plane" is the station.

getQuadFromG2TVolumeId volume_idquad(0-3) = A,B,C,D, else -1 AGML places the pentagons in rotation order A,D,C,B, so this applies the permutation {0,3,2,1}. Measured 16 of 16 (station, pentagon) cells.
getLayerFromG2TVolumeId volume_idlayer(1-2), else -1 The two gas layers of a pentagon: 1 = front foil, 2 = back.
getOrientationFromG2TVolumeId volume_idkFttVertical or kFttHorizontal, else -1 Even volume_id = vertical/X strips, odd = horizontal/Y.

Which of the two gas foils   cm   each pentagon holds two gas layers 1.1682 cm apart, and every point sits on one of them
foilZOffset (static data) -double = 0.5841 [cm] Half the separation of the two gas volumes, from StgmGeo1.xml's PENT_hcdz/2 + PENT_dz + PENT_gdz/2.
layerFromOrientation (static) orientation (kFttVertical / kFttHorizontal / kFttDiagonalV / kFttDiagonalH) layer 1 or 2; -1 if unknown V and DiagonalV share a foil (layer 2); H and DiagonalH share the other (layer 1). The diagonal strips are a second strip layer etched on the SAME foil, not a separate chamber.
layerZOffset (static) layer(1-2)double [cm], signed; 0 if out of range Layer 1 at -0.5841, layer 2 at +0.5841, in the pentagon's own frame. Measured over all 16 (station, quadrant) cells.

agmlQuadLocalToDbLocal
dbLocalToAgmlQuadLocal		 plane(0-3), quad(0-3), xyz[3] (caller's units)bool; xyz[3] Rotates between the AGML quadrant frame and StFttDb's. A pure frame rotation, no translation (measured as exactly 0 in all 16 cells), so the map is its own inverse and the second name is only for readability. REQUIRES geometryFromDb() and refuses otherwise -- in hardcoded mode the two origins are 8-48 mm apart. Does not rescale, so do not mix the cm of quadToGlobal with the mm of getGloablOffset.
Strip geometry   quadrant-local mm. rows 0-2 are XY strips, rows 3-4 are the diagonals; strip is 0-based and bounded by the loaded map.
stripCenter row(0-4), strip, orientationbool; center [mm] Strip centre. A lookup, not a formula: the XY and diagonal offsets differ, so no single added constant fixes both.
stripEdges row(0-4), strip, orientation bool; center, leftEdge, rightEdge, length [mm] Everything hardwareMap() puts on a raw hit, in one call. Edges are diagonals only; XY strips keep -1. Lets a maker that builds raw hits outside the DAQ path fill the same fields the same way.
stripFromLocal row(0-4), orientation, precisionCoord [mm] bool; strip, offsetInStrip [mm] The inverse of stripCenter, off the same maps, so it round trips by construction rather than by two formulas agreeing.
stripSegment row(0-4), strip, orientationbool; x1,y1,x2,y2 [mm] The strip as a line segment in the quadrant.
stripsCross rowA, stripA, orientA, rowB, stripB, orientB, tolerance [mm] bool; crossX, crossY [mm] True if the two strips cross within both of their lengths. tolerance widens each segment at both ends, for the strip width and for a cluster position not being exactly its max-ADC strip centre.

reverseHardwareMap
(two overloads)		 plane(0-3), quad(0-3), row(0-4), strip, orientation (in/out)
or just row(0-4), strip		 bool; rob(1-16), feb, vmm, ch(0-63), orientation
or feb, vmm, ch		 The inverse of hardwareMap(): which electronics channel reads a given strip. Needed to build raw hits outside the DAQ path, e.g. in the slow simulator. rob = quad + plane*4 + 1; feb, vmm and ch are as the fttHardwareMap DB table numbers them.
